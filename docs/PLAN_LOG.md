## Historical QA and design notes

### Mobile and reconnaissance release: 0.6.0

TDD evidence: the initial integrated red gate exposed recovery collisions/orbiting, absent status/presentation modules and insufficient zoom. The independent vehicle author did not read production, qualified the original 28 failures, and later confirmed all 38 original tests passed across 96 recovery frames. The acceptance file's SHA-256 was unchanged between red, green and the final checkout. Additional observed red regressions covered an aged-track firing bearing, short-screen sonar geometry, photo-renderer cleanup, a moving search square, mission-scene replacement synchronous shutter capture and briefing-only save preservation. The final canonical gate passes 181 tests plus syntax/static-build checks. Existing pause, camera, acoustic, damage and mission coverage remains required.

Browser evidence: 393 × 852 has a 524-pixel scene without the ROV warning, visible OFF text, 44-pixel vertical-slider hit width and no page overflow. A physical slider drag changed requested speed to 9.6 knots while actual speed followed gradually. Portrait 320 × 568 and landscape 852 × 393 also fit without page scrolling; compact sonar uses contact codes instead of overlapping long labels. The desktop 1440 × 900 view fits its footer and retains internally scrollable side panels. A 1.2-second contact press survived telemetry refreshes; firing reduced torpedoes from six to five. The surface gun fired in storm conditions. A surfaced boat continued at three knots while its ROV completed recall. At 15.5 knots, the global red warning reported a 34-meter link and the ten-knot catch limit. Inspection showed the gray screw and cable attached to the hull hatch. Chart zoom reached 128× with a 25-meter grid; tower binoculars reached 12×.

Recon/photography evidence: a deep request visibly queued for uplink, then progressed through retasking, exposures and reception. A clear-day plate captured at 2m 58s arrived at 3m 28s with four surface tracks; the 640 × 640 image and scope exposure survived reload. The overhead wave aliasing was corrected to use a mean sea tone at the plate's resolution, preserving scene materials on success/failure. Switching from survey to reconnaissance initially threw on a stale casualty reference and stopped the simulation; after the fix, the same renderer continued advancing. Simultaneous F + 7 initially left a shutter request unarchived; replaying it after synchronous capture increased the archive from three to four immediately. A lost patrol displayed cracks, red tint and 36 rising bubbles behind its report. Sound stayed OFF throughout this release's browser QA. The original exception remains in session log history, with no additional instance after correction. Opening and closing a second briefing-only tab preserved the existing patrol at 23.85 seconds, its 9.6-knot request and all four archived images. The public Pages build loaded the fixed mobile controls, migrated a saved patrol and remained muted/paused. Initial feature deployment passed all 179 checks in [Actions run 44574796333](https://github.com/pmarreck/quiet_depths/actions/runs/44574796333); the save guard adds two conservation checks to the final suite. Hardware iPhone/Safari, subjective sound mix and calibrated historical/radio performance remain unverified.

Delivery uses the existing public GitHub repository and GitHub Pages. No new Codex visualization, ZIP deliverable or private staging deployment is part of this release. The 1972 real-time reconnaissance prototype is a user-approved fictional exception; communication limits and imagery timestamps remain explicit. The existing DU ammunition exception remains.

The design centers on uncertainty: observation should improve a player's picture while exposing the boat. Short patrols should have a clear objective and an extraction, with simple helm orders and time compression for transit.

Initial prototype evidence: a reconnaissance patrol returned to extraction in 13m 52s of simulated time with both photos and full hull health. A separate exposed patrol provoked escort pursuit, depth charges and flooding while its drone was deployed; the drone returned successfully. Save/resume preserved its remote state. Sound activation and all four stations ran without browser error or warning logs. That version passed 16 simulation checks and JavaScript syntax checks. Historical fidelity, difficulty balance, mobile touch ergonomics and audio mix quality have not been independently validated.

### Environment and sound

Environment browser evidence: inspected clear daylight, night running lights and rainy storm seas; observed a continuing patrol change from Dusk to Night. The soundings strip reported 230m water depth and 213m below the keel at 12m reference depth. WebAudio created five nearby engine sources. Distinct storm events produced a 14.894s surface thunder path with a 3200Hz cutoff and a 5.837s submerged path with a 665Hz cutoff. Those events had different source locations; the depth comparison is established by the independent model checks, not that pair of observations. An intentional exposed/pinging patrol provoked charge patterns and nearby blast damage; the final acoustic receipt was a blast with a 0.036s travel time and strong near-field gain. The patrol was lost at 3m 43s. No browser errors or warnings were logged. Sound routing and timing were checked; the mix was not independently auditioned or calibrated. Transient lightning sources and thunder arrivals were verified through the simulation and browser event state; a bolt screenshot is not used as an acceptance oracle.

Earlier deferred design choices, some addressed below: campaign progression, variable mission layouts, calibrated acoustic propagation and temperature layers, crew specialties and repair choices, full conning-tower binoculars, and a balance pass based on actual play sessions. Nuclear propulsion remains excluded because it removes the recharge vulnerability at the center of the design.
